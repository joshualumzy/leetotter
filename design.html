Leetotter

A tutor for the NeetCode 150 that practises like a real interview.

Practising on NeetCode with an AI in the sidebar works, but it forgets what you learned. Nothing picks your next problem, revisits weak spots, or shows your progress. Leetotter takes its place during a session, remembers how you did, and plans what to practise next with little extra work.

What it does

Practise solving, explaining your approach, and facing problems you have not seen before.

Lets you think first

The plan shows a problem's title, never its category or pattern before you solve it. The tutor gives no hint until you ask.

Notices when you’re stuck

After 15 minutes on your own, it can nudge you if you haven't gotten anywhere. A nudge contains no hint.

Figures out the gap

It reads your code and what you say, then helps with the idea, a missing tool, the Python, or a bug.

Brings problems back

A problem comes back if you haven't solved it on your own yet. When a category gets rusty, it usually tests the same pattern with a new problem.

Checks new problems

The plan mixes categories so you have to work out the approach. A new or very weak category may get one or two problems in a row first. It sometimes adds a problem from NeetCode’s extra problems beyond the 150 to test a pattern you need.

Shows where you stand

The category map shows what you have solved on your own and how you handle new problems and complexity. Open a category to see which of its patterns you've already used on a new problem.

What 100% means

All 150 need an accepted submission written in a session. Each problem’s latest graded attempt must reach an approach interviewers usually expect, without a snippet, partial logic or full solution. Questions, examples, analogies and a taught tool are fine. Brute force alone does not count when a better approach is usually expected. A one-off trick is never required. Each category must also be strong for solving and explaining complexity on new problems: at least 21 days’ stability, a latest attempt that wasn’t missed, and a solid attempt on a Medium or Hard problem.

Two AI models grade each attempt independently; if they disagree, the stricter grade stays. You can correct a grade. Ending mid-problem leaves it unfinished.

Your settings, your data

You can change the 15-minute solving time, the five-minute quiet time and gap as one setting, and the chime.

On your Mac, Leetotter runs as a Chrome extension and a small background program that starts at login. The AI models run on the provider’s servers; during a session they receive the problem, your code and messages, and a progress summary. One file on your Mac holds your sessions, how each attempt was judged, your progress, short notes on how you learn, problem references, and settings. Your API key stays in the macOS Keychain.

Built to be trusted

You control the help

Hints need your yes. It watches only during sessions, and every observation it records about you must point to specific moments from your sessions.

Short problem references

There is no detailed answer book or solution code. The tutor and grader work live from the problem and your code, like a strong AI assistant. Each problem has a short reference to its main approaches, time and space complexity, and what interviewers usually expect. A second AI model checks it, so the same approach is judged the same way. Your approach can be different.

Visible failures

If it cannot read the editor, see a result, reach a model, or judge an attempt, the panel tells you. An attempt it cannot judge stays ungraded for you to review.

Where it’s at

Leetotter is a personal project in development. The NeetCode experience comes first; LeetCode support follows later.
